## Architecture and objective findings

1. A learned three-way gate trained with the old flat/spatial objective learned
   wait only: 0% conditional card/placement accuracy.
2. Independent hierarchical decision/card/tile loss recovered card and tile
   learning, but the decision class still needed play-vs-wait balancing.
3. Full inverse decision balancing plus two isolated gate calibration epochs
   reached 19.9% held-out play recall and 79.2% wait recall, but free-running
   gameplay remained off-distribution (2-10 in the six-strategy smoke).
4. A 24,576-row strategy-labeled DAgger set showed the actual mismatch:
   behavior played 7.2% while the teacher labeled 49.2% plays.
5. Training on DAgger alone overcorrected (88.3% play recall, 7.7% wait recall).
6. Mixing the DAgger set with the original corpus at about 1:5 and updating only
   the mode gate reached 52.9% play recall and 84.8% wait recall while changing
   exactly four authorized gate tensors.
7. The F3 shared card pointer improved conditional card accuracy from 50.1% to
   62.3% on the replay-mix split, but a fresh one-epoch recurrent policy no-oped
   in all 12 free-running games. Teacher-forced metrics did not transfer; F3 is
   rejected at this stage and needs its own on-policy initialization curriculum.

## Gameplay evidence

The current F1 development candidate scored 4-8 in a matched two-game screen
against each of the six strategy bots. Mean placement rate across workloads was
7.9%. It went 2-0 against spell-control, 1-1 against slow-push and split-lane,
and 0-2 against bridge-pressure, reactive-defense and balanced. This is an
improvement over its pre-DAGGER 2-10 screen, but it is not competent enough for
promotion.

A tactical oracle DAgger wave is ready:

- `datasets/derived/fresh_current_client_typed_v1_seed1065401/onpolicy_oracle_dagger_6k.npz`
- SHA-256: `91bba2b8608a4a4339e498028a2c0b2e25217e7300f42795aeab2dfe2c335146`
- 6,144 decisions, 22 episodes, all six strategy opponents
- oracle play labels: 24.79%; preceding behavior plays: 7.20%
- depth 2, four simulations, 16 sampled actions; measured probe throughput was
  about 56 decisions/s with four CPU workers.

The oracle layer was subsequently mixed into the 146k corpus at a 4.0% dose and
tested as two separate updates:

- **mode-gate only:** accepted as the new development parent. On the original
  12-game paired screen it improved 4-8/−6 crowns to 6-6/0 crowns with two
  win flips and no loss flips. On 24 fresh-seed games it improved the prior
  parent from 8-16/−11 crowns to 9-15/−5 crowns; one balanced loss flipped to a
  win, no win flipped to a loss, and every workload crown edge was equal or
  better. Placement rate was 8.96% and playable no-op 8.45%.
- **random safety:** on 12 matched random-opponent games, the parent was 4-8
  with −4 crowns and the oracle gate was 5-7 with −2 crowns. One loss flipped
  to a win, no win flipped to a loss, and placement rate stayed essentially
  unchanged (8.2% to 8.4%).
- **card/tile only:** rejected before gameplay. On the same held-out oracle-mix
  split, exact tile accuracy fell from 9.12% to 2.95% and within-one fell from
  13.95% to 11.65%. A lower total loss did not justify the spatial regression.

The next phase is a broader paired evaluation of the oracle-gate development
parent, followed by the gamma-correct three-arm PPO screen only if the broader
gate passes. F3 remains quarantined until it has its own on-policy recurrent
initialization curriculum.
