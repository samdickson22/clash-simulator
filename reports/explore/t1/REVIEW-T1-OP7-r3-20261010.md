**Focus 2: can the N2 starting credit be abused?**
- **In-block CPU is still charged.** A process alive at block start keeps its `last` entry, so every in-block delta of its own is charged. Its seeded credit only matters when it exits, and then only to discount the parent's cutime jump, which contains that same pre-block CPU.
- **PID reuse is safe.** Credit and `last` are keyed by generation `(pid, start_ticks)`, so a reused PID gets no inherited credit. The parent lookup goes through the *previous* snapshot and requires `parent.start ≤ child.start`. All three PID-reuse probes are exact.
- **The one abuse path is R3-2.** When a block-start process is orphaned, its full lifetime credit reaches a surviving budgeted ancestor as debt, and for 3 scans it can absorb that ancestor's unobserved CPU.

**Focus 3: double counting after expiry.**
- **It happens, and it errs toward stopping:** late reap at s+3 gives 200 vs 100, and the race gives 250 vs 150.
- **In practice it's essentially unreachable.** `processes()` keeps zombies, since their stat stays readable, so a child only disappears once it's reaped. At that point the parent's cutime has already advanced, and the only lag is the ≤1-scan scan-order race. A real "delayed wait after expiry" can't separate the two.
- **I accept it.** The scan-race case additionally needs a PID wrap.

## 3. Findings

### R3-1. MEDIUM: N1 expiry can still be renewed, contrary to the ruling, the code comment and the docs
- **Where:** `cpu_accounting.py:35-39`, `:80-86` and `:57-65`. The claim appears at `cpu_accounting.py:35-36` ("Fresh exits cannot renew an older orphan credit and mask unrelated short-lived child CPU forever"), in OP7-T1.md ("fresh exits cannot renew older stale debt") and in OP7-R3-RESPONSE.
- **Impact:** practical risk is low (see the end of this finding), but the ruled fix does not do what it claims. Two independent paths cause this.

**(a) FIFO payment renews stale debt.**
- **Mechanism:** the discount loop pays the *oldest* lot first. When an observed child exits and is reaped promptly, its fresh lot is matched by paying off the stale orphan lot, and the stale *amount* survives under the fresh lot's later expiry. Any parent that has an observed child exit at least every 2 scans keeps its stale excess indefinitely. The excess is capped at the size of the recurring lots.
- **Probe:** a stale excess of 100, then 50 observed exits over about 100 scans, then an unobserved 100. Charged 7600 vs true 7700 in both meters. With 0 to 50 intervening exits, the stale 100 always survives.
- **Fix:** pay lots newest-first. That way any remainder is always the oldest debt and expires on schedule.
  ```python
  for amount,end in reversed(self.debt_lots.get(key, [])): ...
  self.debt_lots[key]=lots[::-1]
  ```

**(b) Expired debt is still forwarded through `credit`.**
- **Mechanism:** expiry removes the amount from `debt[key]` but leaves it in `credit[key]`. When `key` later exits, its whole credit, stale part included, becomes a *fresh* 3-scan lot on the grandparent.
- **Probe:** P auto-reaps C (SIGCHLD ignored), so C's 100 never reaches P's cutime and the lot expires. Ten scans later P exits, and an unobserved 100 lands on G. Charged 100 vs true 200.
- **Side effect of the fix:** after a synthetic late reap, the credit currently holds the child twice. Removing the expired amount from credit restores consistency: debt that expired is credit that will never show up in this process's totals.
- **Fix:** at the expiry step, before pruning:
  ```python
  expired=sum(a for a,e in lots if e<=self.scan)
  if expired and key in self.credit: self.credit[key]-=expired
  ```

**Combined fix, verified in a scratch copy:**
- Both probes become exact: 7700/7700 and 200/200.
- Every §2 row is unchanged, except one synthetic case: "late reap after expiry, then parent exit" goes from 200 (an over-count offset by an absorption) to 300, a clean fail-closed over-count.
- test_op7, test_op6, test_op4 and test_reduce: 122 passed.
- Both edits only remove credit, so they can only increase charges. That makes them strictly fail-closed.

**Required tests (both meters):**
- the FIFO-renewal probe above, which must charge 7700;
- the auto-reap forwarding probe, which must charge 200.

**Severity rationale:**
- **What's under-counted:** a one-shot amount, at most `min(excess, recurring observed-exit credit)`.
- **When:** only after an orphan event, meaning a parent and child vanish in the same interval without a wait, or a parent that auto-reaps.
- **Why it's still a condition:** it's descriptive-flag-relevant, and it breaks a guarantee the ruling asked for and the proofs will advertise. So it is a condition, not a disclosure.

### R3-2. LOW (fail-open, bounded, documentation): N2 enlarges the in-window orphan residual
- **Where:** `cpu_accounting.py:29-31`, transferred at `:57-65`.
- **Mechanism:** a block-start process's seeded credit is its whole pre-block lifetime. If it's orphaned (P and C vanish together without a wait), that whole amount reaches the deepest surviving ancestor as debt. Under v1, only CPU charged in the block could do that.
- **Probe:** with 100000 ticks of pre-block credit, an unobserved 400 inside the window is charged 0.
- **Bounds:** at most 3 scans (about 3–4 s of wall time), and only for unobserved children reaped by that ancestor. With the R3-1 fix it really is bounded at 3 scans.
- **Plausible ssh case:** session teardown under a mux sshd. `bash` plus a job alive at block start die together on SIGHUP, and the surviving `sshd: …@notty` then reaps short copier channels.
- **Fix:** documentation only. The OP7-T1.md residual should say the absorbed amount can include pre-block lifetime CPU of block-start processes, and isn't bounded by CPU charged in the block.

### R3-3. LOW (availability, fail-closed; outside ruling scope): persistent host sample trackers have no starting credit
- **Where:** `supervise.py:50`, unchanged in this delta: `Accounting(before, sample_born, 'ssh_budget')` and the apt equivalent.
- **Effect:** a child alive at supervisor start that is reaped later is charged its pre-start lifetime in **one ~1 s sample**. The sample cap is 150%, so roughly 1.5 s of pre-start CPU reaped at once stops the host.
- **Scenario:** a phase launches while apt-daily-upgrade is running, and `sh apt.systemd.daily` reaps `unattended-upgrade`. That's a lower bar than the 10%/60 s average that motivated N2.
- **Correction of my r2 review:** r2 §2 N2 said "The host sample tracker is not affected"; that was imprecise. It is affected once, for processes alive at supervisor start. OP7-T1.md does disclose this ("the persistent host sample can still charge pre-supervisor child CPU").
- **Recommendation:** pass `baseline_all=True` at both constructions on `supervise.py:50`. It's a one-argument change, purely subtractive, and has the same semantics as N2. Coordinator's call; it doesn't block admission.

### R3-4. INFO (fail-closed): a begin-row dropped from one scan loses its N2 exclusion
- **When:** the generation/ppid/UID recheck in `host_audit.py:69` fails once.
- **Effect:** the row goes `gone`, which forwards its credit as a 3-scan lot. When it reappears it has no previous entry and `start < born`, so the gap CPU is lost. Its credit then restarts at 0, so its pre-block lifetime is charged at reap.
- **Probe:** 620 vs true 100. v1 behaved the same way.
- **Likely sources:** an apt method changing UID during the scan, but those are born in the block, so begin-rows are a theoretical case. One sentence in OP7-T1.md, next to the N6 paragraph, would cover it.
