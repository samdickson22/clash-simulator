# Independent review of K anytime-W (2026-10-10)

**Verdict: CONFIRM_WITH_CAVEATS.** The K4/K4h advance and "do not kill anytime W" both stand. But the 4-thread
win is calibrated to fleet cores, and the planned X stage 3 measures nothing. Everything was checked read-only
against the 3,000 raw game JSONs on 127x03 (`reporting/` plus `input-01/`), the runtime `repo/`, and the reducer
re-run locally.

## 1. K4 = K4h = KU = 116/600: a real coincidence, not leakage
- **The games differ.** Command-sequence SHA-256 values match in only 23/600 seeds for K4–KU, 1/600 for K4–K4h
  and 1/600 for K4h–KU. Per-seed outcomes are discordant in 140 (K4/KU), 156 (K4/K4h) and 164 (K4h/KU) seeds.
  - The discordant splits are exactly 70/70, 78/78 and 82/82.
  - Losses by seat differ: 58/58, 60/56 and 57/59.
- **Probability.** If the three arms were exchangeable within a seed, all three totals would match by chance about
  **0.4%** of the time (Monte Carlo). That's improbable, but I found no mechanism.
- **Bookkeeping is clean:**
  - `cohort` equals `metadata.arm` for all 3,000 games;
  - there's one file per (arm, seed);
  - `loss` is consistent with winner and seat, and there are no draws;
  - the reducer keys on `cohort`.
- **K4h really ran h80.** All four `schedule.json` files carry config SHA `b2a21a90…`, the same as freeze
  0f2a9ec8. The runtime `plan.json` has K4h `coarse_horizon: 80`. Runtime `run.py` and `planner.py` are
  byte-identical to the commit, and `run.py` passes `spec['coarse_horizon']` into `anytime()`.
  - Behavioural fingerprint: K4h cuts off less often than K4 (3.95% [3.52, 4.40] vs 4.54% [3.99, 5.15]), and its
    trajectories diverge from K4's almost everywhere.
  - **Caveat:** the game JSONs don't record `threads` or `coarse_horizon`, so I couldn't confirm h80 from the
    game logs themselves. Record both per game in future.
- **Read "retention 100%" and "K4h = K4" as CI statements.** K4h−K4 is 0 pp with a 95% CI of [−4, +4]. The exact
  tie is luck, so it isn't evidence that h80 is free.

## 2. K1's 96.5% loss is genuine, and it's a regime effect, not a bug
- In 83k of 94k cut decisions, `completed=4`: exactly WAIT, the 2400 alias, and 2401/2402. A cutoff therefore
  always returns a wait.
- **Waiting spirals.** Elixir fills, so cut decisions have 24 candidates, against a median of 12 for uncut K4
  decisions. That raises the cutoff rate further: 96.5% for K1, above KU's 64% of decisions over 200 ms.
- In K1's 21 wins, per-game cutoff rates still ranged from 43% to 99%.
- The plan predicted 30–36% loss for K1. WAIT-first is only a safe default when cutoffs are rare.

## 3. Pairing, seeds and CIs: pass
- Seeds are `4503601407370496 + [0, 600)`, contiguous, with seats alternating.
- All five arms share each seed's decks, seat **and host** (400 seeds on 03, 200 on 01).
- The design has 50 (matchup, seat) cells × 12 seeds. K4/K4h use 5-core affinity masks; the 1-thread arms use 1.
- The bootstrap is one shared 5,000-sample paired resample (seed 2026101002). It reproduces every reported CI
  exactly; an independent 20k-sample bootstrap agrees within 0.2 pp.

## 4. Latency: thin headroom, plus a cutoff-return tail
- **Uncut K4 decisions:** wall p50/p95/p99 = 113 / 165 / 184 ms; median CPU/wall = 2.07, so 4 threads are about
  52% efficient.
- **The reported p99 of 193 ms is the cutoff clamp, not search time.** The real problem is the return path:
  - **10.5% of K4 cut decisions exceed 200 ms** (11.5% for K4h); p99 of cut decisions is 246 ms.
  - Eleven K4 cut decisions took 0.5–10.4 s, and K4h's worst took 22.5 s. All were on 127x01, which is
    consistent with draining cancelled threads under SCHED_IDLE.
  - The sim applies late actions without penalty, a small optimistic bias.
- **Projection for slower cores.** I scaled every decision's wall time by 1/s, which is a lower bound because the
  WAIT spiral isn't modelled. I cross-checked against KU's full-search times divided by the observed 2.1× speedup.

| Per-core speed vs fleet | K4 cut, lower bound | KU/2.1 | 2 threads (≈1.5×) | 1 core (K1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 4.6% | 8% | ~31% | 96.5% (observed) |
| 0.8 | **13.7%** | 20% | ~55% | ~100% |
| 0.7 | **27%** | 33% | ~64% | ~100% |
| 0.6 | **49%** | 50% | ~68% | ~100% |

At 0.6–0.8×, roughly 1.4–5% of decisions would also miss 200 ms through the return tail.
- K1 shows that with a WAIT default, loss is *non-linear* in the cutoff rate. **So K4's advance holds only if the
  Mac delivers ≥0.9× fleet per-core speed on 4 uncontended cores.** Mac E4 v2 must measure this with perception
  running, and it should treat the cutoff default as an open variable.

## 5. X stage 3: the current design is degenerate. Replace it before any reporting game
- In `anytime()`, a cutoff before the full coarse scan keeps **only** wait scores. As a result:
  - `coarse_order` has no effect on what K1 returns;
  - `refine_proposals` only matters after a full scan, which K1 reaches in about 3.5% of decisions.
- Student-hooked K1 is therefore plain K1, plus student inference inside the timer. Both arms would lose about
  96%, and every arm would fail "upper CI < 0" whatever the student's quality.
- **The other options also fail:**
  - *Student-hooked K1 vs K0* has the same inert hooks, and the arm is certain to be killed.
  - *A K2 comparator* needs a planner change (`threads ∈ {1, 4}`) and re-qualification, still cuts off ~31% at
    fleet speed (more on the Mac), and its hooks still act only through refinement.
  - *Dropping stage 3* loses the one deployment-relevant question.

**Recommended freeze: "S-default K1".** This is the 1-core configuration the Mac falls back to when it lacks
4 free cores, and cutoffs are where a student can actually change actions.
- **Arms.** All play on the same 600 paired seeds `4503601517370496 + [0, 600)`, vs v1, at 1 core, 200 ms with an
  8 ms reserve:
  - **C-v1:** K1 ordering, but a cutoff with no complete play score returns v1's polled action instead of WAIT;
  - **S-n:** identical, except it returns the survivor's argmax over legal candidates (WAIT allowed). The student
    runs first, inside the timer, and also sets both hooks;
  - **K0:** a paired descriptive anchor on the same seeds.
  - If any refined play completed, every arm keeps the frozen best-complete-score rule.
- **Contrast and kill rule.** The contrast is loss(S-n) − loss(C-v1), killed if the upper paired 95% CI is ≥ 0.
  Report S-n − K0 descriptively. Use one shared paired bootstrap with the existing seed.
- **Qualify before freezing:**
  - with no deadline, both arms reproduce K1 125/125;
  - injected-clock tests show a pre-scan cutoff returns the student's or v1's action, and that inference time is
    charged to the timer;
  - record `threads`, `coarse_horizon` and `default_source` in every game JSON.
- **Cost:** about 8.5 CPU-h per 600-game arm.
- **Optional secondary:** S-default inside K4 at the same seeds, to cover the 0.6–0.8× regime where K4 cuts off
  13–49%.
