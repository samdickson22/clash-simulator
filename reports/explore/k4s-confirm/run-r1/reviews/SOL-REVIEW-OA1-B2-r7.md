13. **MINOR — The operator health projection drops the new fatal error.** `oa1/mirror_k4s.py:385`, interacting with `tools/k4s_health.py:61`.

**Scenario:** A non-lifecycle failure occurs before the first pass, such as an initial admission push failure or startup-verification I/O error. The new handler correctly returns 2, writes `fatal_error` in raw copy-health, and attempts own-receipt revocation. When revocation succeeds, the final raw record retains that error with `revoked=true` and `final=true`. However, k4s_health.hub() projects only utc/listed/durable_on_hub/ack_returned/pending_return/error/block_errors/lifecycle_error. It discards `fatal_error`. The operator's sealed health output therefore omits the reason the copier stopped and presents both projected error fields as null.

**Independent evidence:** Actual main() controls reproduced exit 2 with fatal_error for registration-push OSError, startup-verification OSError, unexpected listing LookupError, and initial self-identity OSError. The first three completed ownership-checked revocation and preserved the fatal error in final raw health. I then executed actual k4s_health.hub(), with only operational receipt contents substituted in memory, on the reproduced startup-failure record. Its result had `copy_health.error=null`, `copy_health.lifecycle_error=null`, no fatal_error field, and `copier_receipt_active=false`.

**Severity rationale:** The process status and raw health are honest; the copier stops, no lifecycle failure is downgraded, and the projected counters are unknown rather than falsely successful. This is loss of the failure reason in the operator summary, not an unsafe restart or successful-copy claim. It does not invoke the coordinator's new-MAJOR stop condition.

**Fix:** Preserve the fatal reason in the health projection. A fix confined to the new copier handler can populate the already-projected `error` field alongside `fatal_error`. Alternatively, explicitly add `fatal_error` to the health tool's projection. Verify that a fatal start with successful revocation still exits 2 and that its reason appears in actual hub() output; a subsequent lifecycle revocation failure must remain exit 3 with both reasons.
