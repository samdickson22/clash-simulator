## 1. Where Astra was right and I change position

1. **Event/hazard timing is not a drop-in likelihood. [F]** `PolicyConfig.__post_init__` (model.py:191–205) requires `memory_kind="structured"` and `hierarchical_mode_gate_enabled` for `deterministic_hierarchy="event"`, forbids the deterministic resource tracker alongside it, and `act()` forces plays through an accumulated hazard gate; `ppo_update` carries `hazard_conditioned_rollouts`. My Round 1 config (LSTM + event hierarchy + tracker) would not even construct. **Retracted.** Day one uses the categorical `slot` hierarchy with stochastic sampling in rollouts and identical factorized likelihood in updates; `play-gate` aggregation is an evaluation-decoding choice only, declared and held fixed across comparisons.
2. **Levels are wired into the model but into no trainer. [F]** `RolloutBatch`, `_empty_rollout_arrays`, `_sequence_inputs`, `parallel_rollout`, `imitation._batch_inputs`, and `eval._policy_step` contain no `entity_levels`; grep returns nothing. Public contract v3 is therefore a model-only feature today. Day-one requirement, as Astra said, and I add own-hand card levels (Astra's point that body level cannot tell the actor how strong an undeployed card is).
3. **Previous rewards leak into the actor for the exact domain. [F]** `collect_rollout` zeroes them only for causal domains (train_recurrent.py:714–719; model.py:2392–2400). Zero them for every actor domain in the new lineage; they are unavailable at deployment and are a reward channel into recurrent state.
4. **The deterministic public tracker is a structured-memory feature. [F]** `StructuredPublicStateTracker` is invoked only inside the structured-cell branch (model.py:1738–1770). Under LSTM my proposed tracker would be inert. **Retracted.** Explicit public history (`public_history_slots=4`, `public_seen_card_slots=8`) supplies the confirmed-play tracker as actor inputs; the exact cycle cell becomes a later ablation.
5. **The current bad-decision metric enforces exact winners. [F]** `calibration_decisions.root_metrics` sets `bad_decision` if any scalar-best candidate has `native_outcome != scalar_outcome`, even when rankings agree (the coordinator's illustration reproduces this). Both of us want regret-based badness with winner mismatch demoted to a diagnostic. The frozen module stays untouched; a versioned `calibration_decisions_v2` implements the new semantics.
6. **Deck splits do not group derivatives with parents. [F]** `split_curriculum` shuffles within archetype; `CurriculumDeck.parent` exists but is unused there. Fix before any split is used for evaluation claims.
7. **Truncation semantics. [F]** `compute_gae` has one `dones` array and bootstraps only at the rollout boundary. Match end at tick 6000 is a true terminal, so standard games are fine, but `defense_scenario` horizons are truncations scored as terminals. Disable defense scenarios in the pilot and add a `truncated` flag before any scenario curriculum.
8. **Cadence and discounting.** I accept five ticks (250 ms) as primary per `docs/design/PIPELINE_DESIGN.md`, with ten ticks as a sensitivity arm. Astra's gamma point stands: 0.995 at 250 ms is a ~50 s horizon. Proposal: gamma 0.999 primary (~250 s, one match), gamma 1.0 episodic control, 0.995 ablation.
9. **Ordering of fitting versus the gate.** I proposed allowing non-promotable development fitting before Tier A. Since a bounded Tier A costs ~6–7 emulator-hours and the engineering prerequisites (§4 Q6) take longer than that, I concede the ordering: no fitting before Tier A, provided the emulator restart is authorized when the protocol is frozen **[D]**. If Sam declines the restart, the question reopens; I would then ask for a written exception for a ≤100k-decision correctness smoke whose checkpoints are deleted, not a standing waiver.

Also confirmed: `train_dagger_oracle.py` imports the deprecated raster `legacy_model.MaskedPolicyValueNet`; the structured-contract oracle path is `imitation.py collect` (`_collect_oracle_shard` with `FixedDepthThompsonOracle`, behavior-checkpoint mixing via `--behavior-checkpoint`/`--expert-probability`) plus `fit`. `online_strategy_teacher_loss` already supervises decision/card/tile from a `StrategyBot` on learner states inside PPO with a public-legality check, so Astra's scripted warm start has two ready mechanisms (offline BC corpus, online teacher term).

## 2. Where I still disagree, and how to settle it

**Warm-start source.** Astra: public-script demonstrations. Me: privileged fixed-depth oracle labels on student-visited states (DAgger). Astra's own principal uncertainty is that scripts may not supply enough exploration to escape their limits; that is precisely what a lookahead teacher addresses, and it is what Boney et al.'s oracle/follower result supports. But my oracle is shallow (defaults: depth 6–10 decisions, 32–48 simulations, random-sampled action subsets, potential-based leaf), privileged, and its strength is unmeasured. Neither of us can decide this by argument. **Settlement:** measure teacher strength as players first (64 paired games each: `PublicScriptedOpponent` styles, `StrategyBot` styles, oracle-as-player). Then run *both* seeds as arms in the pilot plus a no-warm-start control. If the oracle does not beat the best script as a player, drop the oracle arm. My prior: the oracle wins as a player and its DAgger seed produces a less passive student **[H1]**; Astra's skepticism is recorded.

**Actor observation contract.** Astra: the reference-observable subset from day one. Me (Round 1): exact simulator features first, degrade later. On inspection the deployable set is also the *verifiable* set: the native public projection (`native_public_observation.py`) labels identity, owner, position, HP fraction, level and static lookups, and the real-play contract v2 (`REAL_PLAY_ENTITY_FEATURE_INDICES`: 0–9, 23–28, 30; globals 0–5, 8–13, 17) is the same family. Neither labels status flags, deploy timers or shields. I concede: day-one actor entity features are the real-play v2 subset plus levels, with clean values and confidence 1 from the simulator, so every actor input is coverable by Tier A/B checks. Condition: the plan must name a contract-v4 extension track for visible statuses (stun/slow/freeze/deploy shadow), because the pilot actor will be blind to them while the oracle teacher is not; this is a known handicap, recorded, not silently accepted.

**Event-time likelihood is not "later, maybe".** Categorical wait/play at fixed cadence bakes the cadence into every checkpoint and makes wait rows ~98% of decisions at 250 ms. I want a proper continuous-time likelihood (`event_policy.continuous_time_action_nll`, already implemented as a standalone module) wired into both trainers *without* the structured-memory coupling, scheduled as the first post-pilot engineering item, with the 5-vs-10-tick invariance test as its acceptance. Astra placed cadence comparison "later"; I want it dated.

**Thresholds.** Astra's provisional numbers (0.5 match-score units averaged across two continuations; 5% of initial Crown HP, ≈546 HP at level 11, as tie-break regret) are defensible engineering values but were chosen, not measured. `docs/design/CALIBRATION_PROTOCOL.md` asks that thresholds come from development variance. **Settlement:** compute the distribution of same-outcome HP regret and score deltas from the opened v5–v7 development branches (design use only), set the tie-break regret at a declared percentile, freeze, then collect. If Astra prefers fixed numbers, I accept theirs as provisional with one addition: a **systematic-bias criterion** (below), because Sam asked us to catch systematic small errors, and a material-failure count alone cannot.

**Modest-dynamics randomization.** Astra: only after measuring plausible ranges. I agree on the ranges, but not on deferral: the ranges can be set now from the same opened residual audits (tower-HP checkpoint deltas, timing deltas), and level randomization needs no measurement at all. One arm each; not the primary.

## 3. What the compute grant changes, and what it does not

- **Changes.** The pilot becomes a replicated factorial with a decision rule instead of one sequential run; evaluation blocks reach PIPELINE_DESIGN-grade sizes (hundreds of paired games per cell) and three training replicas per arm; the throughput track (exact resident tensor engine and simple gym on CUDA, differential against scalar) runs in parallel rather than after; and the second experiment, reacting-branch expert iteration using `counterfactual_policy_iteration.kl_regularized_candidate_target`, becomes affordable as soon as Tier B admits it.
- **Does not change. [F]** The Python scalar engine is CPU-bound and per-core speed dominates: the capacity study measured a six-vCPU rented pod at ~280 row-ticks/s against the M4's ~1,650, so one 64-vCPU pod is roughly one Mac mini (~300 learner decisions/s end to end, ≈1M decisions/hour, ≈900 games/hour/seat at five ticks) **[H4, measure in M0]**. The simple gym on an A6000 gave ~400 transitions/s end-to-end PPO at 48/64 capacity, about 1.3× a Mac, is not byte-identical to the scalar oracle, and its 48-entity capacity fails the 33-deck pool. Native reference collection stays on the Mac emulator unless cloud ARM emulation is separately verified (do not assume). Bulk human-video extraction (GH200, ~143 games/hour) is blocked by HUD-variant recognition and a content-policy question, not by money. Engineering bandwidth is now the binding constraint, so arms are ordered by implementation cost, not by compute.

## 4. Proposed common strategy (`council-plan-v1`), by the coordinator's questions

**Q1 Architecture, initialization, reuse.** `ClasherPolicy` fresh: d_model 128, 4 heads, actor 4 layers, critic 2, LSTM 256, `card_semantics_version=4`, `canonical_lane_globals=True`, `public_history_slots=4`, `public_seen_card_slots=8`, `public_entity_levels=True`, hierarchy `slot`, every adapter/prototype/hazard/deterministic-gate/action-value option off. Software reuse: `rl/model.py`, `structured_obs.py`, `public_observation.py`, `public_policy_contract.py`, `public_action_mask.py`, `train_recurrent.py`, `parallel_rollout.py`, `imitation.py`, `eval.py`, `strategy_bots.py`, `public_scripted_opponent.py`, `oracle_planner.py`, `deck_curriculum.py`, `reward_model.py`, `compare_reacting_public_branches.py`, `public_reference_checks.py`, `calibration_families.py`. No historical weights: the promoted champion encodes a passive prior (93% no-op when playable) on a different actor domain; old corpora (`teacher_broad_v1`, `dagger_v2_*`, camera corpora) are earlier contract versions and are regenerated or re-projected, never reused as-is.

**Q2 Demonstration source.** Temporary warm starts are in-simulator teachers: public scripts and the privileged oracle, both labeled "engineering teachers", neither "human strategic supervision". Human data: Astra's one-working-day audit, made concrete: over `katacr_hog26_human_v1` (347 episodes) and `human_safety_fullhuman_tvtrain` (794 episodes) plus saved video, count sequences with an authoritative HUD-confirmed play, observable state aligned within one 10 Hz frame, public mask accepting the action, and both perspectives grouped. If ≥20 exist, human data enters as a human-likeness evaluation set and a low-weight `CausalDecisionRehearsal` prior in one arm; otherwise evaluation only. IL_Replay stays read-only for deck/co-occurrence and coverage planning with forms retained. KataCR's own 0–10% win rates against a fixed opponent are the evidence that video imitation alone will not deliver competence.

**Q3 Day-one fixes versus later.** Day one: entity and own-hand levels through every path (`RolloutBatch`, `_stack_step_inputs`, `_sequence_inputs`, `shared_rollout_ipc.build_rollout_field_specs`, `imitation._batch_inputs/_sequence_batch_inputs`, `PublicPolicySequence.ARRAY_DTYPES`, `eval._policy_step`), a `hand_levels` array from `StructuredObservationBuilder`; simulator level plumbing (`PlayerState` card/tower levels, `apply_ordered_deck_to_player(levels=...)`, `BattleState.deploy_card` → `_create_card_stats_from_data(level=...)`, tower stats via `tournament_tower_stat` at level, `SelfPlayBattleEnv` per-episode sampling with level 11 default); previous rewards zeroed for all actor domains; real-play v2 entity/global subset with confidence channels; masks from `PublicActionMaskBuilder` only; rollouts sample the full factorized distribution and updates recompute the identical likelihood with the same masks; deterministic evaluation decode declared (with stochastic decode reported as secondary); tests for behavior/update log-prob equality on the Python backend (exists only for the simple backend), burn-in versus full-sequence likelihood, terminal versus truncation bootstrap, episode reset, critic isolation, equal-public-history/different-hidden-state invariance. Later: continuous-time event likelihood (first post-pilot item), status-flag contract v4, observation degradation, exact cycle-cell ablation, sub-tile placement residual, capacity scaling.

**Q4 Readiness without a v7 replay.** A separately versioned `training-readiness-v2` protocol, frozen with hashes before collection, granting only the declared scalar/public-policy scope. Tier A follows Astra's shape: 32 independent fresh families on the 16-card roster across tactical situations and both seats, four declared candidates per root (including wait and a public-script baseline), two frozen reacting continuation styles, 256 branches per backend. Criteria: (a) material failure = scalar-preferred candidate loses ≥0.5 match-score units averaged over both continuations versus the reference-best, or exceeds the tie-break HP regret; zero material failures required; zero failures in 32 gives a one-sided 95% bound of ~8.9% on this protocol's failure event only; (b) **systematic-bias criterion**: families with any strict regret ≤ 8 of 32, and per-candidate-type breakdown (wait, play, neighbor, delay) reported, so a consistent small tilt (for example scalar favoring waiting) is flagged even at zero material failures; (c) all public-state checks pass, no declared case missing, missing root = family failure; (d) winner mismatch and trajectory drift retained as diagnostics, never a veto. Cost ≈ 32 × ~12 min ≈ 6–7 emulator-hours on the Mac. Tier B reuses the same machinery on 30 roots drawn from the frozen candidate's own scalar games, stratified by phase/deck/seat, with the candidate's top-2 actions as candidates; required before PPO continuation beyond the pilot, before any search or distillation, and before promotion. Limitations, stated: both bounds cover only the declared failure event under two continuation styles; they say nothing about mechanics outside the 16-card scope or about human sensitivity; opened v5–v7 branches inform thresholds only. v7, its exposure ledger and `calibration_decisions.py` are untouched.

**Q5 Learning and demonstrating adaptation.** Levels: pilot control at 11; one arm samples both seats' card and tower levels from {10, 11, 12} per episode with levels visible (own hand exactly, bodies via `entity_levels`); the scripted opponents stay at 11 (their metadata requires it) unless extended. Demonstration: a 3×3 learner-level × opponent-level evaluation matrix at 256 paired games per cell, requiring graceful degradation rather than collapse, plus intervention tests (swap own card levels mid-evaluation and require measurable timing/placement changes). Modest dynamics: one arm with a hidden HP/damage jitter on two random cards per side per episode, ranges taken from the opened scalar-versus-native residual audits, fixed within a game, held-out combinations, no dynamics identifier to the actor; success is unchanged level-11 strength within 5 points and improved Tier B ranking consistency **[H2]**.
