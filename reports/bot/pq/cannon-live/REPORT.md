## Identity, conventions and limits

Action = `slot*576 + row*18 + col`; integer tile `(x,y)=(col,row)` has center `(x+.5,y+.5)`. Our frame is 18×32, owner 0 is us, y increases toward the opponent, and **y≥16 is the enemy half**. Left is x<9; right is x≥9. Distances below are Euclidean tiles from the commanded center, towers excluded. “Toward us” means matched enemy y decreased over the preceding roughly half-second. Stationary and new/unmatched bodies do not establish future intent; “crossing” is the y∈[14,18] proximity proxy. Hand columns use the exact memory HUD; replayed tracked hands retain Cannon in the selected slot in every case.

Play-accounting conventions follow `/mpac/sdicks02/repos/clasher-live/reports/live/play-accounting/PLAY-ACCOUNTING.md`: submissions/acknowledgments are not proof of deployment, nearest enemies are contemporary, and aim versus first sighting uses a +.5 cell-center correction. ## What the scored rollouts actually reward

The score is `(balanced native leaf + one learned-opponent leaf)/2`, then the wait bonus and reserve penalty. The native leaf is `0.55*crowns + 0.25*princess pressure + 0.10*king pressure + 0.10*tiebreak - 0.20*early king activation + 0.08*board + 0.12*danger + 0.005*(our elixir - enemy elixir)` (with terminal ±2 overrides). The board term credits surviving body HP/elixir value; danger is proximity-weighted threat to crowns. An initial action also changes all later balanced-script plays in the rollout. These are endpoint values, not a learned long-term value function.

## Diagnosis for the Opus owner

**There are two directly demonstrated implementation causes, plus a rollout/value limitation; a global forward-Cannon rule would hide all three and remove at least one verified defensive pull.**

1. **Simulator placement fidelity: dynamic pocket boundaries ignore the building footprint.** Nine of 14 reconstructed native spawn centers differ from the observed game center. All eight requests with row 20 are simulated at y=20.5, but the game lands them at y=19.5; mem-c4/m50 is also shifted horizontally from x=8.5 to x=7.5. Two other tap-to-body adjustments (world edge / river) are already correctly handled by native anchoring. The frozen engine checks point territory in `native-source/lib.rs:1134` and generic point zones in `native-source/scripts.rs:252`, then chooses a static building anchor in `lib.rs:3647`; that separation is consistent with the observed missing dynamic footprint constraint. This is a generic deployment-resolution contract to investigate, not evidence for a Cannon-specific ban. Changing only the selected Cannon to its real center reverses its comparison against the best defensive placement in **five** roots: c2/m01:3470; c4/m21:2740, m29:3390, m39:2940, m50:3260. Three corrected selected actions also fall below the best waiting key. This is not a full re-run of every pocket candidate under corrected geometry, so it does not prove the eventual post-fix argmax. Some corrections improve the forward score (m51 and m55), which also argues for modeling the placement rather than suppressing it.

2. **Candidate generation: one unequivocal missed better defense.** On the reconstructed mem-c4/m16:3110 root, legal Cannon (16,10) beats the chosen (17,19) by **0.008563** under the existing scorer and unchanged, correctly modeled spawn geometry. It is absent from the generated set, not rejected by a rollout timeout. Its coarse rank would be second, so adding it survives the native top-eight screen and wins the stored-score reduction. Across the 14 roots, the exact best defensive Cannon is missing in 13 generated sets, but in the other 13 roots the selected forward action still beats every defensive placement under the original reconstructed geometry. Thus deficient defensive coverage is real, but cannot by itself explain all 14 choices. The m16 fixture distinguishes proposal coverage from a scoring problem without changing the reward. Candidate identities were not logged live, so historical absence of this exact key is inferred from the concordant replay rather than directly observed.

3. **Short leaf, own continuation and uncertain opponent response explain the remaining preferences.** All 14 forward actions reproduce, but their margins arise from eight-second endpoint objective/board/danger/elixir terms and the later balanced-script sequence. The first c2/m01 forward Cannon is a useful warning against an overly simple “board value” diagnosis: versus the best defense, its board and danger terms are *worse* by 0.001811 and 0.004151, and the modeled elixir advantage of 0.007501 narrowly offsets them.
